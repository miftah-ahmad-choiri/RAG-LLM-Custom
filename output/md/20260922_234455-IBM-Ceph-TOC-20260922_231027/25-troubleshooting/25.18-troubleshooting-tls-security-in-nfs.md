## Troubleshooting TLS security in NFS

Last Updated
: 2026-06-25

Learn how to troubleshoot common TLS-related issues in NFS environments.

This section provides essential troubleshooting guidance for diagnosing and resolving issues that affect Transport Layer Security (TLS) when it is used to secure NFS communication. TLS protects NFS traffic by encrypting data in transit and validating identities during the TLS handshake. When TLS or mutual TLS (mTLS) is misconfigured, NFS clients may fail to mount shares or fall back to unencrypted communication.

Troubleshooting focuses on three key areas: validating, checking, and confirming the TLS configuration.

Validating TLS handshake and certificates
Learn how to identify issues in the TLS handshake, such as invalid or expired certificates, missing CA trust, or incorrect identity fields that prevent secure channel establishment between the NFS client and server.
Checking authentication mode (TLS vs mTLS)
Understand how to verify whether the environment is configured for one-way TLS or mutual TLS (mTLS), and confirm that both the client and server provide certificates as expected during connection setup.
Confirming TLS configuration and transport settings
Review share security (XprtSec) settings, server policies, and client configuration, including Stunnel setups when applicable, to help ensure that TLS is correctly enforced and that NFS traffic is encrypted as intended.

These troubleshooting steps help isolate TLS negotiation issues, validate certificate and authentication settings, and ensure secure, encrypted NFS communication in your Ceph environment.
